Added hydration (GDD S07/P05) as its own survival system: a water reserve from 0 to 100 with nutrition's bands (above 50 fine, 25–50 thirsty, below 25 dehydrated). It drains slowly at rest, faster when you sprint or are by a fire. Drinking (I) takes a quarter litre at a time, from your packed bottles first and then the canteen, and is absorbed over a few seconds. Water you drink makes your pack lighter.
Canteen (1 L, now in the starter kit, its water counts toward pack weight): stand in the creek or swamp and press I to fill it. Purification tablets (shop, 6 uses) make that water safe after 30 s. Drinking untreated water can make you ill a little later; the illness costs health only through the shared health system, and it passes. Severe dehydration kept up for a while also costs health the same way, and only outside the tutorial.
Effect: low hydration slows stamina recovery, inside the shared floor (GDD 2.8). The HUD has a WATER bar, and the status screen shows hydration with a warning naming the cause ("Dehydrated (hydration 14): stamina recovers slower… Drink (I)"). The L01 tunnel tutorial has no hydration.
How to see it: `npm run check:t103` (6/6 PASS). It sets you dehydrated with a heavy pack, sprints up the trail and measures how much stamina comes back at rest (0.29), drinks with I (8 times, 2 L), then repeats: 0.45, about 1.6 times faster. It also fills the canteen at the creek with a tablet. Captures are in `.ballast/media/t103/`.
Assumptions: a full reserve lasts about 25 minutes of quiet walking. A drink is a quarter litre and 1 L restores 40 hydration. Untreated water makes you ill 60% of the time. You set off with the canteen full of tap water.

## Questions
### How often should drinking untreated stream water make you sick?
Context: if you fill your canteen in the creek without a tablet and drink it, you have a 60% chance of feeling ill about 40 seconds later. You then lose a little health for 45 seconds, roughly an eighth of it, and then it passes. Tablets cost $9 for six and make the water safe after a 30-second wait.
Options: - 60% as now: a real gamble, but rarely deadly
- Always: untreated water is never worth it
- Rarely (about 20%): a small risk you may choose to take when desperate
Going with: 60%, so treating water feels worth the money without making a thirsty mistake fatal.
