- Creatures now learn your routine, but only from what they actually saw. A predator that watches you walk somewhere remembers the legs of that route and which way you went. Walk the same route again in its view and its confidence grows. Once it has watched you use a route twice, it goes and waits on it. It also remembers a camp it saw you set up (a pitched tent or a fire), holds that memory longer, and goes back later to check it.
- It can't anticipate what it never saw. A route you take behind it, or out of its sight, leaves it nothing to predict, however often you use it. Memories are capped in number and fade: stay out of its sight long enough and it forgets the route and stops waiting there.
- To see it: open the game with ?map=test&dogman. A dark, hunched, upright greybox stand-in with yellow eyes stands watch. Pitch a tent in front of it and walk away, and it comes to inspect the tent later. Walk the same path past it twice and it moves to wait on that path.
- Checked in game:
  - A tent it saw was remembered, and it walked over to check it 45 s later.
  - One walk along a route in its view was learned, but not yet waited on.
  - The same route walked twice behind it was never anticipated.
  - After the second walk in its view, it went and waited on that route.
  - After a long while out of its sight, it forgot the route and gave up waiting.
- Assumptions: it sees 40 m in a 120° cone, blocked by solid objects. Route memories last 7 minutes, camps 10. It waits after 2 witnessed uses. There's no attack yet (greybox): it waits and inspects.
