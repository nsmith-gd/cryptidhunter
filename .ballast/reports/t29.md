- Night-vision goggles (V): the world turns to grainy, scanlined green inside a round tube. You see trees, trails and buildings in the dark with no light at all, but your view narrows from a wide 75° to 50°.
- They run off the same battery pack as the flashlight, and drain it a little faster (about 90 s per battery vs about 120 s). At 0% they switch off, and the R swap works as before. The camera still zooms in when raised through the goggles.
- Cryptids can't see them: the goggles' infrared is invisible, so there's no beam to give you away. Walking straight at a watching Bigfoot, the flashlight got you spotted at about 24 m; with the goggles (torch off) you got to about 5 m, where it saw you by plain sight.
- Bigfoot itself shows as a dark shape against the glowing green ground, which reads as a menacing silhouette at close range.
- To try: `npm run dev`, add `?map=forest`, switch the torch off with F and press V. Captures in `.ballast/media/t29/`: the cabin with the naked eye vs through night vision, the creek trail, and Bigfoot up close.
- Assumptions: they share the flashlight's battery (one battery pack to manage) rather than having their own. They make you no harder to see or hear; they just mean you don't need the torch. The fear meter still treats goggles-only as "in the dark".

## Questions
### Should the night-vision goggles and the flashlight share one battery?
Context: The goggles and the flashlight now draw from the same battery, so wearing the goggles with the torch off still costs charge, and using both at once drains it fast. Spare batteries refill both together.
Options: - One shared battery (current: one thing to manage, the goggles feel costly) - Separate batteries (you can run both; more to track on the HUD) - Goggles with a long built-in charge (night vision is the "safe" option, the torch is the cheap one)
Going with: One shared battery; it makes choosing between light (visible, wide) and night vision (hidden, narrow) a real trade-off.
