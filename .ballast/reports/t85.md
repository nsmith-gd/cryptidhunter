STATUS: DONE
- Every photo you take is now checked for what it really shows and saved as an evidence record. When you press the shutter, the game judges how well lit the subject is (from your torch, lamps and fires, not from how the screen looks), whether you held still, how much of it is in the frame and unobstructed, whether its head and features can be made out, and when and where you took it. Those combine into a quality score out of 100, as the design document describes.
- In the field you only see what a photographer would know went wrong: "Too dark", "Blurred: hold still", "Head not in the shot", "Too small in the frame", or that it looks good. No species name and no score, because the film is only judged once it's developed. The objective banner no longer shows a score either.
- Footprint and scratch-mark photos go through the same checks: the print must really be in the shot, and close and from above beats far and low. A photo of deer tracks stays on the roll but isn't sold as evidence.
- Several frames of the same encounter count as one sighting; meeting him again minutes later is a new one.
- Storage and review: drive home and every record on the roll goes into your evidence archive, kept between nights. Base camp shows the count and your best quality. Get caught and the roll is lost. The end-of-hunt photo album now lists each frame's evidence: its type, quality, four small bars (clarity, identification, completeness, context) and its main problem.
- To see it: hunt Bigfoot in Pine Hollow and photograph him with the torch on and off, then photograph a footprint up close and from a distance; drive home and look at the album. Captures in this task's folder.
- Assumptions: grades, verification at base camp and the money a record earns belong to the evidence-grading and settlement tasks running now; selling photos still works as before. When it's too dark to see him, the photo also loses most of its "identification" points, not just clarity; otherwise a near-black frame still scored as "compelling". The banner still tells you when you've photographed your target, because the hunt needs that to send you back to the van.
