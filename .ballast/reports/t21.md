- Bigfoot now actually hears and sees you instead of bolting at a fixed distance. It bolts once it has noticed you, and a small bar above its head fills as it does: grey when it hears you, amber when it can see you, red when it has spotted you.
- Hearing: the louder you move and the closer you are, the faster it notices. Sprinting carries about 28 m to Bigfoot's keen ears, walking about 13 m, crouching about 4 m, and standing still is silent.
- Sight: it looks where it's facing (a wide cone ahead) and can't see through boxes. In the dark you're only visible up close; with the flashlight on you're visible across about 30 m; crouching makes you harder to see. Shining the beam straight on it gets noticed from any direction.
- Measured run-ups straight at a watching Bigfoot: crouching with the light off got within about 2 m; walking with the light off about 5 m; walking with the light on was spotted at about 24 m; sprinting with the light on at about 21 m (sprinting closes the gap faster). From behind, a sprint is still heard at about 17 m.
- To see it: `npm run dev`, play, and watch the bar over Bigfoot's head and the "hear / see / aware" numbers in the bottom-left. Screenshots in `.ballast/media/t21/`.

### Should players see a "noticing you" meter above the cryptid?
Context: A small bar now floats over Bigfoot's head. It fills as it hears or sees you and turns from grey to amber to red. It makes sneaking up readable, but it's a game-y overlay in a game going for cinematic dread.
Options: - Keep the meter (clear and fair, stealth-game feel) - Hide it and rely on body language and sound (more immersive, harder to read) - Keep it only on easier difficulty
Going with: Keep it for now; at greybox it's the clearest way to feel the senses working, and it can be swapped for animation and sound cues later.
