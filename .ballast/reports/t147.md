Built Level 9, the Pine Barrens, as a greybox, plus the Jersey Devil as an aerial proxy. The map is flat sand under pitch pines whose crowns make a roof. Open sky shows through four barrens, seven canopy gaps along the sand road and in the woods, the cedar bog, and the clearings round the old iron-furnace ruins and a sandstone outcrop. There's a nest of six tall dead pines in the north and the road camp in the south. Trip cost $15,000, payout cap $125,000.
The Jersey Devil patrols the sky. It sees only by sight: never through the canopy, never through rock. In the open it sees a torch from 140 m, movement from 55 m, and someone standing still from 22 m. Your fires in the open draw it to circle overhead.
Every swoop is signalled: it drops to hover over you, shrieks ("A shriek right overhead… Get under the pines NOW!"), and its eyes flare red for about 2.5 s before it dives. Stay in the open and it rakes you (a bleeding wound through the health system); get under the trees in time and it aborts. After a swoop it perches on a dead pine for 7 s, which is the tag window. The tagging contract loans a tag launcher with two tracking tags (slot 4, Q), which tags it within 30 m with a clear shot.
How to see it: `?map=pinebarrens`. `npm run check:t147` (8/8 PASS) shows it blind to you under the canopy and behind rock, seeing light then motion then stillness, signalling then hitting you in the open, aborting when you duck under the pines, circling a fire, and being tagged on its perch. Captures are in `.ballast/media/t147/`.

## Questions
### How long should the warning before a Jersey Devil swoop last?
Context: when it has you in the open, it drops lower, hangs over you shrieking with its eyes flaring red, then dives at where you are. Right now that warning lasts about 2.5 seconds, enough to run a few metres into the trees from the edge of a clearing, but not from the middle.
Options: - 2.5 s as now: the middle of a big clearing is a real danger
- 4 s: you can usually make the trees from anywhere
- 1.5 s: only staying near cover keeps you safe
Going with: 2.5 s, which makes clearings feel dangerous while still giving a fair warning.
