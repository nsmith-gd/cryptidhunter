- The rules for catching a creature alive now exist, shared by every capture level: Free → Eligible (the creature gives a clear opening) → Committed → Secured (restrained on the spot) → Contained (in a crate) → Staged (loaded for transport) → Delivered.
- Committing uses exactly one charge of your capture module, whether it works or not, and only during a real opening. Pressing it with no opening costs nothing, and a missed opening costs only time. Whether it works is decided by plain conditions, never chance: the opening must still be open, the module must suit that creature, and it must be working.
- A failed capture always puts the creature back where it would plausibly be. A worn-down animal is recovering, an aggressive one that's still able turns on you, and the rest flee. An emergency release is always available before delivery and never locks you in.
- Holding a creature has two gauges. Containment integrity warns below 40 and it escapes at 0. Specimen condition warns below 35, and at 0 it can no longer be delivered (no pay for a failed specimen). Safe, suitable containment slowly steadies condition. The crate must suit the creature and be big enough, the transport must take the weight and have a route, and the contract pays exactly once. The creature stays one individual throughout and never becomes a pack item.
- This is the rules layer only, with nothing new to see in game yet. The capture gym (the next task) puts it in your hands. Captures: a state diagram and four scenario traces (success, missed and then late commit, escape, emergency release).
- Assumptions: strain while held is fastest restrained on the spot (2 per second), slower in the crate and on transport, scaled by how hard the creature resists. Adverse exposure such as cold costs 1.5 condition per second. These are tuning placeholders.
