The Hodag is in. It's a squat, horned, low-poly creature with a row of back spines, defending the claw-marked ground in the G04 gym: `?map=g04`, or `&proxy=territory` for the Hodag alone. It doesn't hunt you; it escalates through one readable sequence.
Warning: step into its ring and it rears up, its spines stand on end and its eyes glow red. It growls every second and a half, with a caption saying where the sound is ("close, ahead"). Step back past the stumps and it settles. Stay put for 4 seconds, or walk into its core (you still get at least 2 seconds of warning), and it bellows and charges fast.
If the charge misses, it gives a short pursuit, faster than you walk but slower than you sprint, for at most 6 seconds. A hit gores you for serious, heavy bleeding (bandage with B), and then it turns home with a snort. Ducking behind solid cover makes it lose you.
Leaving the zone ends the pursuit every time: once you're 2 m past the stumps it stops at once, and it never crosses that line itself. The in-game check proved this in 12 escapes (six directions, walking and sprinting), and the unit tests in 48 more. Run `npm run check:t142` (all 6 checks pass). The G04 gym's own checks still pass with the Hodag in place, and captures are in `.ballast/media/t142/`.
Assumptions: the sounds are synthesised placeholders (growl, roar, footfalls, snort) until there's real audio. The Hodag currently lives only in the G04 gym; a Level 6 greybox forest to put it in is separate work. Its numbers (15 damage plus heavy bleeding, 6.5 m/s charge, 3.8 m/s pursuit) are first guesses.

## Questions
### How far past the claw-marked stumps should you need to get before the Hodag gives up?
Context: The stumps mark the edge of its ground. Right now it keeps chasing until you're 2 metres beyond them, then stops dead and turns home with a snort, every time. That makes running for the stumps a reliable escape.
Options: - Keep 2 m past the stumps: clear and reliable
- Exactly at the stumps: even more readable, slightly more forgiving
- A bigger buffer (5–6 m): scarier, and a walking player gets caught more often
Going with: 2 m, so the stumps are a line you can trust but you still have to commit to getting clear.
