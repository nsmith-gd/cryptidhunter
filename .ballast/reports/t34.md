- The master palette is now a set of swatch files you can open in any paint program: 98 colours in 15 hue-shifted ramps (bark, wood, needles, moss, grass, dirt, leaf litter, rock, lichen, water, fur, skin, warm light, night, rust). Shadows lean cool and lights lean warm, with no pure black or white. Saved as a GIMP/Aseprite/Krita palette, a plain hex list and JSON, in the art palette folder in the docs.
- Colour-grading ramps for each time of day and biome: forest dusk (warm low sun, violet shadows), forest night (cold moon, deep blue haze, warm lanterns as the only warm colour), forest dawn (pale sky, warm horizon, lifting fog), and swamp dusk/night/dawn with a sickly green-teal haze. Each sets sky, fog, ambient light, key light, accent and shadow tint, all picked from the palette.
- A palette render pass snaps every pixel on screen to the nearest palette colour (matching on hue as well as brightness, so dark greens stay green), with a light pixel dither where gradients would band.
- Test scene: a Pine Hollow diorama rendered in forest dusk, night and dawn and swamp night through that pass. The check reads back every pixel (102,240 per frame): all are palette colours, 32–54 distinct per frame, versus about 4,500 shades without the pass. Captures in `.ballast/media/t34/` (one grade per image, plus an unquantised comparison).
- Not switched on in the game yet: the pixelation pipeline belongs to #35 and lighting to #36, and either can add the pass in one line.

## Questions
### Should the whole game be locked to the palette on screen?
Context: In the test scene, every pixel is forced to one of the 98 palette colours. Shading becomes stepped and slightly dithered, which reads strongly as pixel art. Without it, lighting and fog produce thousands of in-between shades.
Options: - Lock every frame to the palette (strongest, most consistent pixel-art look) - Palette for textures only, free lighting (smoother, more realistic night) - Lock with a larger palette for night scenes (more steps in the darks)
Going with: built and tested, but left off in the game until the pixelation pipeline (#35) lands. Compare the paired captures to decide.
