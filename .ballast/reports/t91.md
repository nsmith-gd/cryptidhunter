Pine Hollow now has the map pieces the GDD asks of every expedition (sections 12.1–12.3, 13.2, 16.4). The GDD has no Pine Hollow description of its own (Appendix D2), so I checked the map against those general rules.
The trailhead has a roofed map board lit by a hanging lantern, so the start is a warm, safe pool of light, plus a tipped-over trash bin with litter dragged toward the woods.
The map edge is a post-and-wire fence with yellow "PROPERTY LINE" signs. You can see it and bump into it, instead of hitting an invisible wall of trees.
A hunter's tree stand stands just off the trail into Bigfoot's hollow. Walk up its steps to a railed platform 3.2 m up and look over the hollow's clearing.
Bigfoot's signs grow stronger the closer you get to its home. Snapped saplings, and a dropped pack with its flashlight still shining at the west ford. Then claw marks above head height, a stick teepee and a rock cairn. Past the ridge, two saplings bent into an X. In the hollow, a snapped pine and a bed of torn branches. These are scenery only; finding clues still works as before.
Glossy puddles lie in the trail ruts near the creek and in the hollow, catching the moon and your flashlight.
Cost: 16 merged pieces (about 3,000 triangles) and 2 lights. In the slow test browser the frame time matched the forest without them (within 3%). Hitting the GDD's 60 frames per second still needs measuring on a real PC or headset. Before/after shots of every spot are in .ballast/media/t91/.
Not yet improved: the old flat treeline wall shows behind the fence where the trailhead clearing reaches the edge, and the bed of branches in the hollow is hard to see without the flashlight. The Bigfoot reference image applies to the creature, not the map, so it wasn't the bar for this task.
## Questions
### Should the map's edge be a fence, or stay as forest you can't walk through?
Context: Walking to the edge of Pine Hollow used to stop you at an invisible wall in the trees. Now there's a waist-high wire fence with yellow "PROPERTY LINE" signs, so you can see where the hunting ground ends. It's the plainest way to meet the GDD's "map edges are physical and communicated" rule, but it's man-made in a wild forest.
Options: - Keep the fence and signs (clear, a little man-made) - Replace it with a wall of fallen trees and thick brush (wilder, harder to read at night) - Use both: fence near the trailhead and roads, brush everywhere else
Going with: the fence everywhere for now, because it reads clearly in the dark.
