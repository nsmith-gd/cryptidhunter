- Cryptids now run a proper behaviour loop with five moods. Roam: wander. Investigate: walk over to a noise it heard, or to bait, and look around. Observe: stop dead and stare at something it glimpsed. Flee: run once sure it's you. Aggress: come straight at you.
- Bigfoot plays it shy but curious. A brief noise makes it come and check; if it glimpses you it freezes and stares for a few seconds (your photo chance), then bolts once it's sure. Stay hidden and it loses interest and wanders off.
- Each cryptid's temperament and timings come from its own data entry: what it does when it spots you, how loud a noise must be to investigate, how long it stares, how fast and how long it chases. A hostile test cryptid (a short red "Prowler") shows the Aggress side. It only appears with `?sandbox` on the web address: it chases you once it spots you and gives up if you outrun it.
- Debug overlay (on by default, F4 hides it): a panel top-right lists each cryptid's current mood, how long it's been in it, what it hears and sees, and its last few changes with the reason ("investigate → observe (glimpsed you)"). A coloured label floats over each cryptid, and the body tints amber when staring, red when fleeing and orange when attacking.
- To see it: `npm run dev`, play, make a short sprint near Bigfoot, then stand still in the dark and watch the overlay. Screenshots in `.ballast/media/t22/` (investigate, observe, flee, aggress).
- Assumptions: Bigfoot never attacks for now (the hunt stays a photo hunt); the Chupacabra and Mothman keep their own separate placeholder behaviour, and moving them onto this shared brain is future work. All timings are first guesses.

## Questions
### Should Bigfoot ever turn on the player, or always run away?
Context: Right now Bigfoot is purely shy: it investigates noises, stops and stares when it glimpses you, then always flees once it's sure. Touching it still ends the hunt, but it never comes at you on purpose.
Options: - Always flee (current: a pure stealth-and-photo hunt) - Bluff charge if you corner it up close, then flee (a scare moment, rarely deadly) - Real attack if you keep pushing it (adds danger to the starter cryptid)
Going with: Always flee, so the starter hunt stays approachable; a bluff charge is an easy data change later.
