- Creatures can now follow a scent trail. Every blood drop from a wound, every spot where you ate, and every carcass is a real scent mark in the world. Each mark fades with age and drifts downwind. A predator that catches a mark on the wind walks to it, then puts its nose down and goes mark to mark toward the fresher end. It follows the way you actually walked, round corners, not a straight line to you.
- It only knows what's out in the world. Food sealed in your pack leaves nothing, so it notices nothing. The counterplay works: bandage the wound and the blood trail stops, so it reaches the last drop, casts about for a few seconds and loses you. Scent that falls in water is carried off, so wading across a creek breaks the trail. A carcass keeps giving off scent for as long as it lies there.
- To see it: open the game with ?map=test&howler. A brown, box-shaped stand-in predator waits nearby. Get hurt and walk off bleeding, and it comes along your trail. It doesn't attack yet: this task is the nose; the stalk and pursuit belong to the Howler itself.
- Checked in game: sealed food in the pack, nothing noticed. Bleeding along an L-shaped walk left 14 drops; the follower checked all 14, never strayed more than 1.4 m off your route, and went round the corner. After bandaging and walking on, it lost the trail and was 34 m behind.
- Assumptions: a nose bridges gaps of up to 7 m between drops (one drop every 3 s at walking pace is about 4 m apart). It casts about for 8 seconds before giving up. Wind is the existing steady breeze until weather wind exists.
- Captures: a top-down plot of your route, the blood drops and the follower's path (no art target applies, greybox).
