- Rebuilt the pine trees (the lighting is unchanged, as approved). Instead of stacked identical cones, every pine is now grown from rings of real branches around a gently bent trunk. Each branch is a drooping, tapering spray of dense needles with two side sprays forking off it, so the crowns are ragged masses of twigs you can see between, not smooth Christmas-tree shells.
- No more copy-paste: there are now 12 different grown pine shapes and 4 young ones, not 4 near-identical cones:
  - classic and tall columnar firs;
  - broad, full-skirted pines;
  - wind-swept trees with branches longer on one side;
  - sparse old trees with a bare lower trunk and dead branch stubs;
  - broken-topped trees with a side shoot curling up;
  - forked tops.
  On top of that, every tree in the forest leans slightly, is sized and stretched differently, and has its own shade of green (some cooler, some warmer), so neighbours never match.
- To see it: `npm run dev`, open `?map=forest` and look along any trail, or stand under a tree with the flashlight. Before and after pairs (same spots) are in `.ballast/media/t60/`; `06-pine-variants-lineup` shows the variants side by side. The tree lineup is also in the forest look-dev page.
- Quality bar: compared against "slurpee run" (night, warm against cold, crisp silhouettes) and "ex3" (pixel scenery with depth). The treeline silhouettes now read as a real, irregular conifer forest; up close under the torch you see layered needle sprays. Still short of the targets: distant trees can look a little thin where branches turn edge-on, and there's no per-tree moss or lichen yet.

Expected to be fine on a desktop GPU; couldn't measure real frame rate here (software rendering). Forest layout, collisions and the lighting checks are unchanged and pass.

## Questions
### How dense should the pine crowns be?
Context: The pines are now built from individual branches. Up close you can see sky and moonbeams between the branches; at a distance some trees read as fairly open and spiky, especially the sparse old ones.
Options: - Fuller (dense, dark crowns; heavier forest, about 20% more rendering cost) - Current (mixed: some full, some sparse and ragged) - Sparser (open, ghostly old-growth look, see further through the woods)
Going with: Current mix, which gives variety and keeps the cost reasonable.
