- VR controllers can now do everything the keyboard and mouse can (20 actions): move, sprint, crouch, turn, flashlight, swap battery, examine, journal, status, eat, night vision, wood knock, quick slots and using items (bait, trail camera, lure), raise the camera and take photos, drive home, pause and confirm. Each button sends the exact same command the desktop sends, so behaviour can't drift between the two.
- Layouts: two-handed by default (left hand moves and handles torch, camera, examine and journal; right hand turns, teleports, snaps photos, uses items and knocks). There's a one-handed layout (hold grip for the second layer of buttons) and a left-handed mirror.
- Comfort: snap turn of 45° per flick by default (30° optional), with optional smooth turning at an adjustable speed, plus a darkening vignette at the edges while you turn. Push the right stick up to aim a teleport: a green ring marks a spot you can reach; red shows the reason in words ("Path blocked", "Too far", "Can't teleport during an encounter"). Teleporting charges the same time on the night clock and the same cold, wet and hunger as walking there. Seated mode lifts your view and never needs a crouch. No camera shake, head bob or roll in VR. Losing tracking or taking the headset off pauses the hunt.
- Photos in VR are judged from where you point the camera in your hand, not where your head is looking.
- To check: `npm run check:t75` (14/14) runs an emulated XR session with fake controllers; captures are in `.ballast/media/t75/`.
- Works with the new "Enter VR" button (#74): once you're in a headset the controllers take over by themselves, and taking the headset off uses the same safe pause. Controller bindings are first-pass choices.

## Questions
### Should turning in VR default to snap or smooth?
Context: In VR, flicking the right stick turns you in 45° jumps, the usual comfort default. Smooth turning (with dark edges while you turn) is available in settings, but some players find it nauseating.
Options: - Snap 45° by default (current: most comfortable) - Snap 30° by default (finer aim, more flicks) - Smooth by default with the vignette (more immersive, riskier for comfort)
Going with: Snap 45°, the design document's comfort-first default.
